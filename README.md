Project Overview

The Prototype Canteen Analysis Dashboard is an HR analytics project designed to evaluate employee attrition before and after the introduction of a canteen facility.

The organization has a workforce of 2,000 employees across five cities and is experiencing an attrition rate of approximately 10%. Management introduced a canteen facility as an employee-retention initiative, with an expected reduction in attrition to approximately 7%.

The dashboard provides a data-driven view of attrition and helps HR understand how the change varies across cities, employee roles, and months.

Business Problem

The organization needs to evaluate the change in employee attrition after the canteen intervention and understand how attrition varies across different cities and employee roles.

Objective

The objective is to develop an HR analytics dashboard that:

Compares attrition before and after the canteen intervention.
Provides monthly attrition trend analysis.
Analyzes attrition city-wise.
Analyzes attrition role-wise.
Identifies areas with higher attrition.
Assesses the effectiveness of the canteen initiative.
Dashboard KPIs

The dashboard contains four major KPI cards:

1. Total Employees

2,000 employees

2. Current Attrition %

Approximately 10%

This represents the attrition rate before considering the canteen intervention.

3. Post-Canteen Attrition %

Approximately 7%

4. Attrition Reduction

Approximately 3 percentage points

These KPIs provide a quick overview of the overall impact shown in the dashboard.

Key Business Insights
Overall Attrition Improvement

Overall attrition decreased from approximately:

10% → 7%

This represents a 3 percentage-point reduction in the attrition rate.

City-wise Insight

Attrition declined across all five cities following the canteen intervention.

Pune was identified as having the highest post-canteen attrition in the dashboard analysis.

Role-wise Insight

WM Technicians had the highest pre-canteen attrition at 10.20%.

Their attrition decreased to 7.00% after the intervention.

AC Technicians have the highest post-canteen attrition at 7.24%, making them a potential area for additional retention attention.

Most importantly, all four roles experienced a reduction in attrition after the canteen intervention.

Overall Conclusion

The dashboard indicates that employee attrition improved after the introduction of the canteen facility.

The overall attrition rate decreased from approximately 10% to 7%, while the reduction was also observed across the different cities and employee roles.

The role-wise analysis shows that all four roles experienced lower attrition after the intervention, while the city-wise analysis shows a decline across all five cities.

Therefore, the dashboard provides evidence of a positive association between the canteen initiative and improved employee retention.

Note: The dashboard shows an association between the canteen intervention and lower attrition. It does not independently prove that the canteen directly caused the reduction.
